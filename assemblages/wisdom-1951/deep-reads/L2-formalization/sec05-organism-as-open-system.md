# Section 5: The Organism as an Open System

*[read-together — Wisdom 1951 BJPS 2(5), pp. 20–21]*

## 1. The Thermodynamic Incompatibility of Closed Cybernetics

A critical limitation of early servomechanism theory was its tacit reliance on closed Newtonian mechanics:
- In closed systems, classical thermodynamics dictates that free energy decreases monotonically ($dF \le 0$), forcing the system into maximum entropy (static equilibrium, $v = 0$).
- If an organism were a closed system, it could not sustain dynamic "steady motion" (homeostatic hovering).

## 2. Integration with Ludwig von Bertalanffy’s General System Theory

Wisdom bridges cybernetic feedback with von Bertalanffy's 1950 papers on open systems (*Science* 111, *BJPS* 1):
- **Open System Criterion:** An open system maintains continuous inflows and outflows of matter and energy.
- **Equifinality & Steady State:** Bertalanffy proved that open systems can attain a time-independent non-equilibrium steady state ($\dot{x} = 0$ with non-zero flux) that is equifinal—independent of initial conditions.
- **Synthesized Deduction:**
  1. Adaptation is defined as the maintenance of steady motion.
  2. Steady motion cannot exist in closed systems.
  3. Therefore, an adapting organism must be an open thermodynamic system.

## 3. Machine-Addressable Grounding

- **Sign:** `open-system-steady-motion`
- **Sense:** The necessary thermodynamic condition allowing homeostatic and ultrastable feedback loops to preserve non-equilibrium steady states through continuous environmental throughput.
- **Nominatum:** Biological metabolic exchange maintaining negative entropy ($\Delta S < 0$ locally) in living tissues.
