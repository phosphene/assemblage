# L0 Compass — J. O. Wisdom: The Hypothesis of Cybernetics (1951)

## 1. Executive Synopsis & Core Argument

J. O. Wisdom's *"The Hypothesis of Cybernetics"* (BJPS, May 1951) is the earliest rigorous philosophical assessment of cybernetics published in Great Britain. Situated chronologically between Norbert Wiener's *Cybernetics* (1948) and Ross Ashby's *Design for a Brain* (1952), Wisdom strips away the rhetorical, idiosyncratic, and overly dense mathematical obscurities of early cybernetic publications to extract their verifiable scientific core.

Wisdom formulates the central thesis of cybernetics as a two-fold hypothesis:
1. **The Primary Physiological Hypothesis:** The chief operational mechanism of the central nervous system is negative feedback.
2. **The Teleological/Behavioral Hypothesis:** Negative feedback constitutes the sufficient physical explanation for "purposive" and "adaptive" behavior.

By defining purposiveness strictly as **error-compensating self-correction** (where an action's output deviation is tapped to check and redirect input driving power), cybernetics rescues teleology from Aristotelian final causes and vitalism without collapsing into Newtonian clockwork determinism.

Crucially, Wisdom demonstrates that:
- Cybernetic systems are **non-Newtonian machines** that hover in dynamic "steady motion" rather than static mechanical equilibrium.
- Adaptation across changing environments requires Ashby's **ultrastable step-function break**, allowing machines to reorganize coupling topologies and discover attractors unpredicted by their designers (refuting Descartes' causal reality principle).
- Cybernetics **does not solve the Mind-Body problem**. It is an expansion of physical neurology that leaves phenomenal consciousness unaddressed, remaining fundamentally one-sided.

## 2. Epistemic Stakes & Key Interlocutors

| Interlocutor | Citation / Ground | Wisdom's Crucial Dialectical Engagement |
|---|---|---|
| **Norbert Wiener** | *Cybernetics* (1948) | Acknowledged for mathematical formalization and the prediction of cerebellar purpose tremor as feedback hunting; criticized for presentation defects and conflation of mind with mechanism. |
| **Arturo Rosenblueth** | Rosenblueth, Wiener, & Bigelow (1943) | Behavioral definition of purposeful action as teleological feedback; clinical verification via ataxia vs. intention tremor. |
| **W. Ross Ashby** | *Mind* (1947), *Electronic Eng.* (1948) | Elevated as the critical conceptual advance: distinguishing static equilibrium from steady motion, and introducing the Homeostat's step-break uniselectors. |
| **Warren McCulloch & Walter Pitts** | *Bull. Math. Biophysics* (1943, 1947) | Electroneural net logic and the visual scanning transform (superior colliculus scansion of spatial figures into temporal pitch invariants). |
| **Ludwig von Bertalanffy** | *Science* (1950), *BJPS* (1950) | The thermodynamic bridge: organisms as open systems sustaining steady states; closed systems inevitably collapse to static entropic rest. |
| **Kenneth Craik** | *The Nature of Explanation* (1943) | The internal "symbolic model" hypothesis; acknowledged as prescient but criticized as too sketchy and introspective. |
| **René Descartes** | *Meditations* (1641) | Formally refuted: Ashby's homeostat disproves the Cartesian dogma that the cause must contain at least as much perfection/knowledge as the effect. |

## 3. Foundational Conceptual Vocabulary

- **Negative Feedback (Simple):** An operational topology where a portion of driving energy is redirected to check or damp output deviation ($e(t) = y_{\text{desired}} - y(t)$).
- **Amplification Feedback:** A circular loop where the error signal taps an *independent local energy reservoir*, driving high-gain damping or explosive positive runaway spirals.
- **Steady Motion (Steady State):** A non-zero, dynamic state of active error-correction and metabolic throughput, distinct from static thermodynamic rest.
- **Hunting (Purpose Tremor):** Violent dynamic instability arising when feedback correction overshoots or possesses a time lag commensurate with system latency.
- **Ultrastability & Step-Break:** System adaptation characterized by discontinuous step-changes in coupling coefficients whenever essential variables exceed physiological viability bounds.
- **Regenerative Loop:** Lorente de Nó's closed neural circuits circulating discrete action potentials over time, functioning as dynamic memory without localized physical inscriptions.
- **Scansion Transform:** The temporalization of spatial fields, converting metric scale/rotation variance into invariant temporal pulse sequences.
